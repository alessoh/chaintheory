Chain Theory Building Set

Chain Theory, an unproven research program announced by Alex Wissner-Gross in October 2026, says that nothing exists but finitely many maximally entangled pairs of small quantum systems, some of them shared. Here that axiom is a construction set: a ball for every quantum system, a rod for every entangled pair, and the theory's own particle networks assembled from its published data.

The rules

Two rules make it a set

The set has three kinds of ball. A qubit is a quantum system with two basic states and a qutrit has three. A hub is two qubits read as a single piece with four states. A rod is one maximally entangled pair, with a ball at each end. A ball can sit at the end of several rods at once. The theory then carries it as one copy for each rod, so no pair ever has to share its entanglement with a third piece.

The first rule is about sockets. A ball with two, three or four states can end at most two, three or four rods. This is the theory's valence law, a rule its author chose, and it does real work. A qutrit in the electron's triangle spends two sockets on the triangle and has one left. What fills that last socket at each corner is what separates the electron from the muon and the tau.

The second rule is about looking. Multiply the numbers on the two balls of a rod. If the product is six or less, a single look, which the theory calls a read, can pin the pair down. If it is more than six, no single look can. The theory's words for the two kinds are readable and dark, and this page calls them clear and hidden. The threshold is established mathematics. By theorems of Størmer, Woronowicz and Choi, six is the largest product for which one standard test decides whether two systems are entangled. The theory joins that fact to its own definition of a read.

The fourth column is the part of physics that the theory takes each kind of rod to correspond to. Its own data file marks these assignments as conjectured. They show in the models: the gluon is a chain of the strong interaction's rods, the photon a chain of electromagnetism's, and the Higgs a single rod of its own kind.

A hub with its three qubits, two of them tied to each other, is a cluster that the theory calls a hub assembly. It appears again and again: one of them turns an up quark into a down quark, and one more at each step turns an electron into a muon and a muon into a tau.

The hole counts are those of a school molecular model set, where two-hole, three-hole and four-hole balls usually stand for oxygen, nitrogen and carbon. Every model on this page can be built with one, using the short rigid links for clear rods and the long flexible ones for hidden rods. In none of the models here do the clear rods close a ring, so a rigid link never has to bend. This match is the book's illustration and no part of the theory, and the shapes mean nothing: the theory gives its networks no lengths and no angles.

Bench trials

What a rod is

A rod stands for one maximally entangled pair. Three small benches show what that means, using nothing but standard quantum mechanics for the pair.

Look at both ends

Left looks
Right looks

Each end is a qubit. The theory counts three ways of looking at a qubit, which can be pictured as three perpendicular directions. Each end alone answers like a tossed coin. When the two ways match, the two answers are always the same. When they differ, one answer says nothing about the other.

Simulated from the quantum rule for one maximally entangled pair. The way that matches at the far end is the complex conjugate of the near one, as the theory's Letter spells out.

Distill two rods into one

Two pairs: A with B, and C with D.

The axiom allows one operation, called distilling: two pieces are projected onto a pair. Do that to the inner ends of two rods and the outer ends, which never met, are left as a pair as well. Physicists know this as entanglement swapping. It moves entanglement and adds none.

In a laboratory this outcome turns up one time in four for qubits. In the theory the other outcomes are no configuration of the axiom, so the projection is simply the act.

A rod between unlike ends

A qutrit has one state more than a qubit, so a rod between them ties the qubit's two states to two of the qutrit's three and leaves the third untied. Count the ways of relabeling the states at the two ends, three at one and two at the other, with one phase between them. Together they form the symmetry group behind the Standard Model's three forces, in one of the four forms that the known particles allow, and the two tied strands carry the electric charges of an up quark and a down quark.

That much is established group theory. That nature's forces arise this way is the theory's conjecture.

The one number

A count that lands on 137

The theory's best-known result is a number. Take one qutrit with three rods to three qubits. Read two of the rods and leave the third unread. The theory calls this arrangement the fine-structure star, and it counts the ways the two reads can pass through it. There are 172, which it calls records. They fall into four groups, and each record enters the sum with the square root of the product of the ends it passes.

The divisor 31,104 is the size of the space the two reads are taken over: 24 from the rod left unread and 36 from each rod that is read.

The arithmetic is exact and has been checked by machine. What it means is not settled. The theory conjectures that this kind of rod is the elementary link of light and that the number is therefore the inverse of the fine-structure constant α, which sets the strength of electricity and magnetism. The match with the 2022 official value runs to ten digits, and newer measurements will show whether it holds.

The limits

A set with no ruler and no clock

The models come without lengths, masses or times, and the theory's documents print none in ordinary units. Where its counts are read directly as physical sizes, they miss. The table gives four examples, worked through in Appendix F of the book.

So the models are best taken as a picture of what the theory claims and of what it still owes. On counts and patterns they do well, though mostly through results the theory itself marks as conditional: two polarizations of light, whole electric charges built from thirds, a neutral neutron, three generations. A finished theory would have to turn the same counts into the measured numbers.
